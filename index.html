The opportunity

Students need more than theory. Employers need more than résumés.

FORGE closes the gap between learning technology and operating it.

Students get safe, hands-on access to real computing environments. Professors gain facilities for applied research. Local organizations gain a pathway to supervised student talent and affordable technical support.

Canada’s cybersecurity workforce gap is the project’s starting point—not its only purpose. The same infrastructure supports cloud, AI, data and connected-device work.

Delivery with guardrails

Progress is earned at each decision gate.

The project has three formal kill points. Each one requires evidence before the next major commitment.

Kill point 1 · Jun 2028

Design & affordability

Approve design, permits, cost baseline, partner interest and signed UFV bridge commitment before construction.

Kill point 2 · May 2030

Systems & security

Major systems must pass testing, independent security review and defect checks.

Kill point 3 · Aug 2030

Final acceptance

Confirm acceptance criteria, handover documents and the first-year operating budget before opening.

Schedule target: open August 2030, ahead of the September 1, 2030 project deadline. A two-month project buffer is planned for June–July 2030.

Questions funders will ask

The hard questions, answered directly.

What if industry does not commit?

Kill point 1 requires at least 10 partners committed. The team plans to pilot the platform early and recruit partners before construction. If demand or costs fail the criteria, the project can be redirected or stopped.

Can the hub really pay for itself?

The target is $6M in annual revenue against about $5.5M in annual operating costs at full operation. The plan stages growth: the base case targets full coverage by 2032; the conservative case by 2034. The conservative ramp-up forecast includes a $6.6M cumulative shortfall.

A written UFV bridge commitment of up to $7M is a condition before kill point 1. Project contingency cannot cover operating deficits.

How is the $100M protected?

Proposed controls include fixed-price contracts where possible, monthly earned-value reviews, steering committee approval for contingency spending, and three formal go/no-go kill points. A cost performance index below 0.90 triggers a formal review.

What is the return on investment?

This is a public-value proposal, not a commercial payback investment. At a projected $0.5M annual surplus, a simple $100M payback would take about 200 years. The intended return is practical training, research capacity, industry collaboration and long-term operating sustainability.

Where can I review the detailed project plan?

The full 18-page project charter and management pre-plan includes the detailed budget, revenue assumptions, stakeholders, work breakdown structure, technical and legal requirements, schedule, risk register and references.
