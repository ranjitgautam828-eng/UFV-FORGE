The decision requested

Fund the talent pipeline, with the controls to stop it.

We ask for approval of $100M, released against three formal kill points, with a plan for the hub to cover its own operating costs.

$100M

One investment, seven budget lines, 5% contingency.

3 kill points

The funder can stop or rescope at set milestones with evidence in hand.

80% → 100%

Target operating-cost coverage by 2030, then fully by 2032.

Hard questions

What a funder will ask, answered directly.

What if industry partners do not commit?

The pilot review in Dec 2027 tests user demand and partner commitments ahead of the formal design decision in June 2028. If partners or costs fail the criteria, the scope is reduced or the project is halted, before the major construction spend.

What if costs overrun?

Fixed-price contracts where possible, a 5% contingency ($5M) held by the steering committee, and a monthly earned-value review. Monthly earned-value reviews flag cost pressure early; formal go/no-go decisions occur at the three kill points.

Can the hub really pay for itself?

The plan targets $6.0M a year in revenue at full operation against roughly $5.5M in operating costs: memberships $1.5M, sponsored projects $1.2M, grants $1.0M, nonprofit hosting $0.8M, endowment income $0.8M, training $0.7M.

These are targets, not guarantees. The plan assumes 80% cost coverage by 2030 and 100% by 2032, and the first kill point requires at least 10 committed partners and a signed UFV bridge commitment before construction.

Is the demand real?

A 2022 ICTC report found a shortage of about 25,000 cybersecurity professionals in Canada. Lexpert (2025) cites fewer than 4,000 graduates a year against demand above 25,000, and Bell Cyber’s president cites at least 50,000 unfilled roles (CP24, 2026).

How will you report to the funder?

Quarterly progress reports, audits, and grant alignment for government, plus monthly status reporting from the project office. Benefits are reviewed at closeout on September 1, 2030.

Why students first?

Students operate the servers, maintain live sites, and work on real client problems. Faculty chairs, industry matching, and community access follow from that outcome.
