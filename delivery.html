Execution & control

Move fast where possible. Stop where necessary.

A $100M proposal needs clear ownership, measurable thresholds and decisions that can be challenged—not just a timeline.

01 / Govern

One decision owner

The project lead integrates scope, dependencies, risks and status reporting. Larger changes go to the steering committee.

02 / Control

Watch the baseline

Monthly reviews compare actual spending and completed work against the plan. A cost performance index below 0.90 triggers a formal review.

03 / Verify

Test before acceptance

Building inspections, equipment burn-in, load testing, privacy review and independent security testing provide evidence—not assumptions.

Risk watch

What could knock the plan off course?

These are the risks that deserve attention early, while there is still room to change direction.

Industry demand is weaker than expected

Use the platform pilot to test demand and recruit partners before construction. If fewer than 10 partners are committed by the first gate, do not proceed as planned.

Construction costs or permits slip

Use competitive procurement and fixed-price contracts where practical. Start consultation and permit work early, track schedule dependencies, and use the buffer only for critical-path delays.

Security or privacy controls fail

Keep the cyber range isolated from UFV's normal network, apply least-privilege access and logging, complete a privacy impact assessment, and close critical security findings before acceptance.

Revenue ramps up too slowly

Review service mix and costs if coverage is below 40% at the end of 2031. Any temporary operating shortfall must use the proposed UFV bridge commitment—not the project contingency.

Scope expands during delivery

Require written change requests with scope, cost and schedule impacts. Changes over $250,000, or changes affecting a kill-point date, go to the steering committee.
